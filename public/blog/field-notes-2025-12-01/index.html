Retrospective field note for 2025-12-01. Reconstructed in November 2026 from dated sources, not originally published on the archive date.

On 28 November, I was trying to restructure how AEGNTIC, AE Limited, Prompt Fail and the other projects worked together, then make that understandable on a website.

The question wasn’t whether there were enough names. There were plenty. It was what someone should make of the relationships between them.

Opus 4.5 had launched four days earlier. More capable assistance made it easy to imagine another layer. The project map needed a less glamorous next step: decide what each existing part was for.

A research direction, a product, a publication and a reusable capability can share an identity without sharing a runtime. A link between two pages doesn’t mean the tools synchronise. A concept doesn’t become a working feature because it sits next to one.

I’d start with the visitor’s route. What do they come here to do? What can they inspect or try? Where does the result go next? A few clear relationships will explain more than a diagram where every node connects to everything.

The conversation records an attempt to organise the portfolio, not a completed integration. That distinction belongs on the site too. Show the working parts, label the explorations, and stop making people reverse-engineer the ecosystem from its names.
