Retrospective field note for 2025-09-08. Reconstructed in November 2026 from dated sources, not originally published on the archive date.

The LLM.deals brief called for automatic news and blog updates. That raises an immediate problem: ten sites can repeat one announcement and look like ten stories to a careless pipeline.

DeepSeek-V3.1 is still the August release in this week’s context. It hasn’t launched again because another summary appeared.

I’d start with a change record, not an article generator. Keep the original source, retrieve it again, and compare the fields that matter. A changed footer isn’t news. A changed model identifier, access condition or deprecation date might be.

Then ask whether that change affects the directory’s reader. Does it alter which tool fits a task? Does a previous recommendation need correcting? Is the new detail supported by the vendor’s source or only by summaries quoting each other?

The system needs permission to publish nothing. Otherwise a schedule becomes a demand to invent significance every morning.

That is where automation can actually help: gather and compare the evidence before editorial work begins. It can remove repeated checking without removing judgment. I’d rather open the dashboard to one meaningful change than a dozen rewritten headlines. Activity is easy to generate. Attention is the scarce bit.
