A price without its conditions is barely a price

An AI directory that lists “cheap” next to a tool hasn't done the comparison yet.

Field NotesRetrospective

Retrospective field note for 2025-09-15. Reconstructed in November 2026 from dated sources, not originally published on the archive date.

An AI directory that lists “cheap” next to a tool hasn’t done the comparison yet.

The September LLM.deals plan needs prices people can interpret: amount, billing unit, plan, source and checked date. If those details are missing, a tidy comparison table can mislead more efficiently than a messy page.

DeepSeek-V3.1’s August release remains the context here. I’m not presenting it as new news this week. Its presence in a fast-moving market is enough to make freshness part of the directory’s design.

A token price doesn’t tell the whole story. Input length, output length, caching and retries affect the bill. Use a worked example with visible assumptions rather than declare one provider universally cheaper.

Put the checked date beside the information. A disclaimer hidden on another page does nothing for someone choosing from a card. If the source stops responding, keep the last-known value labelled as last-known. Don’t guess the update.

This is unglamorous work, but it’s the work the directory is meant to save its visitors. Anyone can collect prices once. Keeping them interpretable after the next model release is the product.
