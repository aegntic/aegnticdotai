A link should take you to the evidence

Source links need to support the sentence beside them.

Field NotesRetrospective

Retrospective reconstruction of the week beginning 2025-02-10, written on 16 November 2026. This is not a claim of publication at the time.

Following February’s deep research launch, source-backed answers deserve a closer look. Adding links is easy. Making them useful takes a little more care.

Click one. Does it support the claim, or does it land on a broad homepage? Does the page contain the number being quoted? Is that number still attached to its unit and date?

These details matter when research moves into another format. A price copied into a spreadsheet can lose its billing period. A recommendation copied into a project brief can lose the condition that made it reasonable. By the third handoff, nobody remembers the original limitation.

Keep a compact record with each important claim: source, date, relevant detail and any qualification. The reader should be able to check the disputed point without repeating the entire search.

There is no need to show every abandoned lead. Show the evidence that carried the conclusion. If the source is inaccessible or only a summary was available, say so beside that claim. A dead end labelled honestly wastes less time than a citation that only looks convincing.
