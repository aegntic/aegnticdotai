The verdict

What you actually get back.

A single document. No ongoing retainer. Five sections, fixed contract. Written by the Chairman — the final decision-maker of the council — not by a committee.

01

The Decision

A one-line call. Proceed, do not proceed, or proceed-with-conditions. No soft compromise.

02

Consensus

Where the council converged and why. The arguments strong enough to survive deduplication.

03

Strongest Dissent

The single most dangerous objection that survived the collision round. Named, not buried.

04

Biggest Risk

The one failure mode that would invalidate the decision. With the leading indicator to watch.

05

Next 7 Days

A concrete action plan for the first week. Owners, sequencing, kill-criteria. Not a roadmap.

The kind of bet generic advice misses.

A question that looks like a pricing decision — and isn't. Side-by-side: what a competent general-purpose AI or advisor returns, vs what the AE Audits council surfaces in the same 48 hours.

Audit ref · AE-2403-22

"Should I pivot my $4M ARR B2B SaaS from per-seat to usage-based pricing next quarter?"

$4M ARR 8 enterprise renewals due 3 co-founders 60-day window
Submitted by a Series-B founder. Same brief sent to a general-purpose LLM, an external advisor, and AE Audits. Verdicts below.

What generic systems return

"Depends on your churn elasticity. Consider A/B testing."

  • Survey the top 20% of customers by spend
  • Build a usage-metering layer before announcing anything
  • Pilot with 5–10 design-partner accounts for 90 days
  • Model three churn scenarios: 10%, 20%, 30%
  • Communicate clearly; offer grandfathering for 12 months
  • Monitor NRR closely during transition

What AE Audits returns

Do not pivot. The pricing model is not the binding constraint. Three of your eight enterprise renewals carry MFN pricing clauses — any rate change triggers a mandatory re-quote and a 90-day cure window.

  • Hidden constraint: MFN clauses in enterprise contracts. Per-seat → usage is a rate change. Pivot auto-breaches 3 of 8 renewals representing $1.4M of the $4M ARR.
  • Wrong churn model: The 30%-churn-worst-case advisor estimate ignores that the 3 MFN accounts are your highest-LTV logos. Real downside is 35%, concentrated in the worst segment.
  • Operator lens: Your billing system cannot meter usage today. Engineering estimate for a compliant metering layer is 14 weeks, not the 6 the brief assumes. Pivot cannot ship in Q3.
  • Lindy lens: Of 27 documented B2B SaaS per-seat → usage pivots since 2019, 22 were reversed or re-priced within 18 months. The successful 5 added usage as a second tier, not a replacement.
  • Strongest dissent preserved: If a competitor pivots first and you do not, you cede the "modern pricing" narrative to procurement teams. This is a real risk — but it is a marketing risk, not a pricing-engineering risk.
Next 7 days
DAY 1-2Counsel pulls all 8 enterprise contracts. Flag every MFN, price-lock, and change-of-pricing clause.
DAY 330-min call with the 3 MFN accounts. Test willingness to re-paper in exchange for committed-use discounting. Do not mention "usage-based".
DAY 4Engineering scope: how much of the metering layer can ship in 8 weeks if deprioritised against everything else. Honest answer only.
DAY 5Draft the "additive tier" alternative: keep per-seat, layer usage-based add-on for power users. Same revenue thesis, zero contract breach.
DAY 6-7No announcement. No engineering work. Decision re-review at day 30 against MFN-account signal.

Before you submit

Five things worth knowing first.

Expensive, irreversible or hard-to-reverse, and made under real uncertainty. Examples: fork-or-build a product, accept a term sheet, sign a multi-year vendor, fire a co-founder, migrate a production stack, raise a round at a specific valuation, take a strategic investment, ship a security-sensitive feature under deadline. If the cost of being wrong is recoverable in a week, this is overkill.
By contract. The Anti-Caricature rule forces named operators to be used as compressed reasoning lenses, not stylised imitation. The output uses each operator's distinct decision criteria, risk models, and prioritisation logic — not their public speaking quirks. If a section reads like a meme version of the person, it gets cut in the deduplication round.
One paragraph on the decision itself, one paragraph on what you have already ruled out, the constraints (financial, temporal, contractual, relational), the kill-criteria you are currently using, and any data that contradicts your preferred outcome. We send a structured brief template on request. The shorter the brief the sharper the verdict.
Two recourses. First: if the verdict does not surface at least one risk you had not already written down, you do not pay. Second: on Strategic engagements, the defense round exists specifically to push back when you believe the council missed something. The council either defends its position or revises the verdict. Either way, you see the disagreement in writing.
The council is an adversarial reasoning system. The fifteen advisers are reasoning archetypes, not fifteen humans sitting in a room. The Chairman (a human) reviews every verdict before it ships. The value is in the structure — fifteen distinct lenses forced to collide, then deduplicated into one decision-grade document — not in any single voice.
