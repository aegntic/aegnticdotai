> Retrospective field note for 2025-12-15. Reconstructed in November 2026 from dated sources, not originally published on the archive date.

GPT-5.2 was announced on 11 December, with professional work and artifacts prominent in the release. It's easy to see why polished spreadsheets, slides and documents get attention. They look finished.

That makes them worth checking twice: once for the substance, once for the presentation.

A spreadsheet needs its inputs, units and formulas checked. A research document needs the sources to support its claims. A deck needs a story that matches the evidence, not just slides that look related.

Then open the actual export. Inspect page breaks, dense sections and the places most likely to overflow. A successful file conversion tells you the converter ran. It doesn't tell you the table is readable.

Assumptions need somewhere to live. If a comparison depends on a particular input, make it discoverable. If a conclusion is an inference, say so. That's easier to work with than a confident number whose basis vanished during formatting.

I'd rather receive a slightly plainer artifact with traceable claims than an immaculate one that needs to be rebuilt after its first serious question. The appearance gets someone to open it. The substance determines whether they can use it.
