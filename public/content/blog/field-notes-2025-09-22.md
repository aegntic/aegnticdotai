> Retrospective field note for 2025-09-22. Reconstructed in November 2026 from dated sources, not originally published on the archive date.

Automatic blog creation was part of the directory brief. I don't want that to mean a machine producing articles because Tuesday arrived.

Start with the change and the reader. What happened? Who does it affect? What can we establish? If those answers are thin, the post probably is too.

DeepSeek-V3.1's August announcement is a simple example. We can cite the release and explain what to test before switching a workflow. We cannot turn that into a story about a production migration and cost savings that never happened.

A draft should carry its sources and flag anything still needing verification. It should also know whether the same announcement has already been covered. A follow-up needs something new: a result, a comparison, a correction, or a different question worth answering.

Dates need the same care. Writing about an earlier week doesn't make the article a document from that week. This archive is reconstructed; its publication date must say when it was actually published.

Generating the sentences is the easy part. The editorial decision is whether there's something here worth adding to the site. Give the system a perfectly acceptable outcome called “nothing to publish”. That will improve the feed more than another prompt asking for engaging content.
