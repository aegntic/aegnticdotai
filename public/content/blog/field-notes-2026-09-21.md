> Retrospective field note for 2026-09-21, reconstructed on 16 November 2026. The archive date is not a claim of publication at the time.

The user changes direction while the assistant is explaining the original plan. Does the work change, or does the explanation continue?

A live system needs a real interruption path. Stop the discarded work, retain what is still relevant and pass the new instruction to active workers. The person should not have to wait for an obsolete plan to finish before being heard.

The visible states should help. Checking a source, waiting for a service and waiting for the user are different situations. Say which one applies. Constant motion and commentary can conceal a stalled task just as effectively as a blank screen.

Short jobs can often finish quietly. Longer ones need updates when the information helps the person steer or decide. Narrating every tool call mostly consumes attention.

Gemini 3.8 Live adds another implementation to evaluate. Test the interruption and recovery as carefully as the conversation. The assistant should be able to pause, accept a correction and continue without making the user repeat the entire request.

Google released Gemini 3.8 Live on 15 September. [Source](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/changelog).
