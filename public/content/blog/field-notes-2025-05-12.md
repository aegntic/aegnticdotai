*Retrospective reconstruction of the week beginning 2025-05-12, written on 16 November 2026. This is not a claim of publication at the time.*

On 4 May, I wanted a research summary to arrive in an app with a Linux notification. Accepting the idea would start the project setup: instructions, task tooling, memory, documentation, a GitHub repository and the required automation.

I also asked to favour free open models, using paid specialists for specific cases. Gemma 3's March release fits that wider interest in having options. These were workflow requirements, not evidence that all the integrations worked.

The acceptance step is important. Research can collect lots of possibilities without creating a repository for every passing thought. Once an idea is chosen, the setup should carry its problem, evidence and first task into the project.

Documentation belongs in that chain. In the conversation, I corrected the account of some named tools and asked for proper research. Recognising a tool's name is not enough to generate its setup confidently.

There was a design request too: give the resulting apps a consistent identity and connect them through a central website. Reuse the parts that should feel familiar. Save the invention for what each app actually does. Otherwise the automation produces a collection of unrelated starting points that someone still has to join up.
