> Retrospective field note for 2026-07-20, reconstructed on 16 November 2026. The archive date is not a claim of publication at the time.

“What did we decide?” should not produce five links and another research assignment.

Read the matching conversations. Find the correction, the later decision and the reason it changed. Then answer the question, with the relevant reference available for checking.

Chronology matters. An abandoned plan can contain all the right keywords. A draft may look more complete than the short message that replaced it. The assistant has to distinguish exploration from commitment instead of flattening both into memory.

Keep the scope too. A preference for one project may be wrong for another. Private planning material can inform a public draft without being quoted into it. Retrieval access does not make every recovered detail publishable.

Unified search helps locate the material. The remaining job is reading it properly. Carry forward what is settled, avoid reopening the same rejected option, and preserve why the choice was made. If the person still has to reconstruct all of that, search has found the archive but has not recovered the decision.
