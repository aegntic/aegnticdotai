*Retrospective reconstruction of the week beginning 2025-06-02, written on 16 November 2026. This is not a claim of publication at the time.*

On 31 May, I asked for visual examples. The response listed effects. I asked again: every category, and each item should actually show what it describes.

The brief also specified an icy monochrome palette, a muted light-mode version, hover reveals and scroll-triggered steps. It accepted system reduced-motion preferences and a Lighthouse performance target after the graphics were integrated. Requirements, not scores we had achieved.

Claude 4 had just been announced. More implementation could be delegated, but the design choices still needed to be visible before they spread across a product.

Show the hover. Show the transition. Put the font name in the font. A label saying “glass” leaves too much room for a result that technically follows the instruction and still looks wrong.

Test the reduced-motion state alongside the animated one. Keep the information and controls available. Measure performance with the intended graphics present, rather than getting a good number from an empty shell.

This is how a catalogue becomes something you can choose from. If half the examples are descriptions, the person is still doing the visualisation work the catalogue was supposed to handle.
